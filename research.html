Quasisymmetric coinvariants

Symmetric polynomials have an outsized role across mathematics, and play a particularly important role in enumerative geometry and representation theory. Quasisymmetric polynomials are a (purely combinatorial) generalization of symmetric polynomials that have proved unreasonably effective in studying similar problems. My work here focuses on the relationship betwen quasisymmetry, toric geometry, and problems in Schubert calculus.

This includes collaborations with Nantel Bergeron, Philippe Nadeau, Hunter Spink, and Vasu Tewari,

Unipotent combinatorics

In characteristic 0, the basic question in the representation theory of finite groups is to identify the conjugacy classes and irreducible representations of a group. For `the' sylow p-subgroup \(\mathrm{UT}_{n}\) of the general linear group \(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\) over a finite field (the unipotent upper triangular matrices), solving this problem for all n is is certifiably "wild." This program sought to develop a combinatorial representation theory of the groups \(\mathrm{UT}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\), and to relate these combinatorcs to Green's classical combinatorial representation theory of \(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\) and Lustig's newer theory of unipotent representations.
